# Limitation Taxonomy for EdTech Governance Studies

Use this taxonomy to declare limitations precisely. For each limitation, name (1) the claim it weakens, (2) the likely direction of bias, and (3) the evidence that would reduce it.

## 1. Sampling limitations

| Limitation | Typical wording | Claims weakened | Bias direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Convenience/volunteer sample | "Participants were recruited from three partner schools" | Generalization to other regions, school levels, systems | Usually overestimates motivated, tech-ready participants |
| Single-site or single-system study | "Data came from one university" | Cross-context generalizability of governance mechanisms | Ambiguous; context effects unknown |
| Low response rate | "27% of invited teachers responded" | Prevalence and perception estimates; non-response selection | Unknown; often overestimates engaged respondents |
| Attrition | "12% of the cohort dropped out by wave 3" | Longitudinal change and effect estimates | Usually underestimates effects if dropouts are struggling participants |

## 2. Measurement limitations

| Limitation | Typical wording | Claims weakened | Bias direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Self-report only | "Outcomes were self-reported" | Actual behavior, learning or governance performance | Overestimates socially desirable behavior |
| Single-source data | "All variables came from one survey" | Relationship estimates; common-method variance | Inflates correlations |
| Newly developed instrument | "The scale was developed for this study" | Construct validity of the measured concept | Unknown until validation replicates |
| Proxy indicators | "Platform logins measured engagement" | Engagement, competence or effectiveness claims | Overestimates activity-based constructs |

## 3. Design limitations

| Limitation | Typical wording | Claims weakened | Bias direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| No control/comparison | "No comparison group was available" | Causal attribution of effects | Overestimates intervention effects |
| No baseline | "Only post-test data were collected" | Change claims | Overestimates change |
| Short time frame | "Data covered one semester" | Sustainability, institutionalization, long-term effects | Overestimates durability |
| Confounded intervention | "The platform and training were introduced together" | Which component caused the effect | Ambiguous component attribution |
| Sequential qualitative-quantitative integration | "Interviews followed the survey" | Mechanism claims where timing matters | Order effects unknown |

## 4. Analysis limitations

| Limitation | Typical wording | Claims weakened | Bias direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Low statistical power | "The subgroup had 38 cases" | Null results; small effect detection | Null results may be false negatives |
| Unadjusted multiple testing | "Twelve comparisons were tested" | Individual significant results | Inflates false positives |
| Model misspecification | "No covariates were included" | Adjusted relationship estimates | Confounding may inflate or deflate estimates |
| Single-coder qualitative analysis | "One researcher coded the interviews" | Dependability of themes | Coder bias unknown |
| Missing data handling | "Missing cases were listwise deleted" | Sample representativeness and estimates | Biased if missingness is not random |

## 5. Validity-threat limitations (educational intervention context)

- Selection: groups differed before the intervention on relevant characteristics.
- Maturation: change may reflect development, not the intervention.
- History: external events (policy change, pandemic, platform outage) coincide with the study.
- Testing: repeated measurement changed participant behavior.
- Implementation fidelity: the intervention was not delivered as designed.
- Spillover: control participants received the intervention indirectly (e.g., teachers sharing materials).

## Reporting rules

1. Lead with the limitation that most affects the headline claim, not the easiest one to admit.
2. Every limitation sentence must name the weakened claim: "The absence of a comparison group means the improvement cannot be attributed to the intervention."
3. State bias direction when knowable: "Self-reported competence likely overestimates actual skill."
4. Do not use the limitations paragraph to undermine the study wholesale; state what remains defensible.
5. In mixed-methods studies, declare integration-level limitations (e.g., qualitative findings could not explain the quantitative null because interviews ended before the survey analysis was complete).
