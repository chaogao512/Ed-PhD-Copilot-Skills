# Implication Mapping for EdTech Governance Research

Implications must be traceable to findings. Use the mapping below to separate theoretical, practical and policy implications and to reject implication claims the evidence cannot support.

## The mapping rule

Every implication statement must pass three checks:

1. **Anchor**: Which finding (with effect direction and size) supports it?
2. **Boundary**: Under which conditions (population, context, design) does it hold?
3. **Audience**: Who can act on it — researchers, schools/districts, or policy-makers?

If any check fails, downgrade or delete the implication.

## Implication types

### Theoretical implications

- What does the finding say about the theory anchor (supported, refined, contradicted, boundary conditions found)?
- Does the mechanism observed require adding a concept (e.g., time allocation as mediator) to the theory?
- What competing explanation did the design rule out, and what remains untested?
- Example: "The mediation result refines organizational change theory for digital competence by showing that school-level time allocation, not training volume, carries the effect."

### Practical implications

- For whom (teachers, principals, teaching-research groups, IT departments) and under what conditions?
- What concrete practice change follows (design feature, workflow, training structure, support routine)?
- What implementation costs or risks should the practitioner weigh?
- Example: "For districts running teacher digital competence programs, the finding suggests scheduling protected co-planning time rather than adding platform training hours; this implication is bounded to schools with existing peer-collaboration routines."

### Policy implications

- Which specific governance mechanism (standards, data rules, evaluation criteria, funding, accountability) does the finding inform?
- Trace the policy claim to the finding; do not derive policy from policy.
- State the level (national, provincial, district, institutional) and the uncertainty.
- Example: "The log-analysis finding supports including data-minimization requirements in institutional learning-analytics policies; it does not by itself justify a national regulation, because the study covered three universities."

## Implication downgrade rules

| Evidence available | Implication allowed | Implication banned |
|---|---|---|
| Descriptive survey | Needs, perceptions, acceptance statements | Effectiveness claims for interventions |
| Pre-post without comparison | Feasibility and participant response | Attributing change to the intervention |
| Quasi-experiment with baseline equivalence | Context-bounded effectiveness under stated limitations | Universal effectiveness across contexts |
| RCT or strong matched comparison | Causal effect for the studied population and conditions | Mechanism claims without process evidence |
| Qualitative single case | Mechanism hypotheses and transferability questions | Statistical generalization |
| Log data only | Behavioral patterns | Educational value or learning claims |

## Structural output

For each implication produce a row:

| # | Implication | Type | Anchor finding | Boundary conditions | Audience | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ... | Theoretical | ... | ... | Researchers | High/Medium/Low |

Then derive the Discussion paragraph from the rows, ordered from most to least evidence-anchored.

## Anti-patterns

- [ ] Implication states "should strengthen X" with no finding anchor.
- [ ] Policy implication restates the policy document instead of the finding.
- [ ] Practical implication ignores implementation cost or risk.
- [ ] Theoretical implication is a new hypothesis presented as a finding.
- [ ] Implication generalizes beyond the boundary conditions stated in the limitations.
