# Governance Discussion Drafter

## Overview

Draft a Discussion section that converts empirical or design findings into defensible scholarly claims for education technology and governance papers. Replace vague summary ("this study found...") and promotional implications ("this will promote digital transformation") with a structured argument: what was found, what it means against theory and prior evidence, why unexpected results occurred, what the study cannot claim, and what researchers, schools and policy-makers may or may not infer.

## Handbook Guidance

Consult `handbook/03_Paper_Writing/3.5_写作细节与Checklist.md` for writing-level standards that complement this skill's Discussion structure.

## Reference Navigation

- Read `references/discussion-patterns.md` before drafting any paragraph.
- Read `references/limitation-taxonomy.md` when writing the limitations paragraph.
- Read `references/implication-mapping.md` when deriving theoretical, practical or policy implications.
- Read `references/worked-examples.md` when the user needs filled Discussion examples for mechanism or empirical studies.

## Required Inputs

Ask for missing items: research questions, headline findings (with effect direction and size), theory anchors used, comparison literature (verified sources only), method and sampling details, evidence boundaries already established by the analysis, and target venue/manuscript type (CSSCI journal, SSCI journal, doctoral chapter).

## Core Procedure

1. Restate the research question and the core findings without repeating the Results section.
2. Interpret findings against the theory anchors; state where evidence supports, refines or contradicts the theory.
3. Compare with prior literature (verified sources only); name agreements and disagreements explicitly.
4. Explain unexpected or null results with plausible, evidence-linked reasons; do not invent post-hoc stories.
5. Declare limitations using `references/limitation-taxonomy.md`; map each limitation to the claims it weakens.
6. Derive implications using `references/implication-mapping.md`; separate theoretical, practical and policy implications.
7. Close with what this study adds and the next research step, not with slogans.

## Quality Rules

- Never introduce new findings in the Discussion.
- Never claim implications beyond the evidence boundary set by the method (see `references/implication-mapping.md`).
- Every limitation must name the affected claim and, where possible, the direction of its likely bias.
- No promotional language: "significant", "revolutionary" and "comprehensive" without evidence are banned.
- Policy implications must be traceable to findings, not to the policy documents themselves.

## Output Format

1. Discussion skeleton with one-sentence function per paragraph
2. Finding-to-theory interpretation table
3. Literature comparison table (with verified citations only)
4. Limitations table (limitation → weakened claim → likely bias direction)
5. Implication mapping (theoretical / practical / policy, each with evidence basis)
6. Closing paragraph and next-research-step suggestion
